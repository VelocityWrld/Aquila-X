# Noise and Vibration

## Dampeners

Dampeners can reduce vibration transmission from motors and propellers to the airframe, camera, sensors, and flight-control electronics. Candidate measures include rubber dampeners or silicone grommets, shock-absorbing motor mounts, foam isolation around cameras and sensors, and shock-absorbing landing gear where terrain requires it.

Dampeners add mass and can introduce unwanted flexibility or resonance if selected or installed poorly. Their stiffness, placement, preload, environmental durability, and effect on control-loop behavior should be tested rather than assumed.

## Acoustic Reduction Concepts

The source material proposes low-RPM, large-diameter propellers, scimitar-like blade geometry, composite materials, balanced propulsion assemblies, smooth aerodynamic surfaces, and possible ducting. It also proposes lower-KV motors, sinusoidal or field-oriented ESC control, and soft-start or soft-stop behavior. These measures involve trade-offs in mass, thrust, efficiency, cooling, controllability, and manufacturability.

## Stealth Mode Concept

A software-defined quiet-flight mode could reduce speed, climb rate, power transients, and other contributors to acoustic output within safe operating limits. It should never override minimum altitude, obstacle-clearance, battery reserve, airspace, or emergency constraints.

## Testing

Noise and vibration should be measured using calibrated accelerometers, microphones, and frequency analysis across representative motor speeds, propeller configurations, payloads, and mounting arrangements. Test results should record environmental conditions, distance, orientation, RPM, thrust, power draw, vibration spectra, and control performance.

## Source Context

The source material describes noise reduction as important for video stability, sensor quality, flight precision, and operator comfort. It also links vibration control to protection of cameras, sensors, and onboard computing. These are design considerations requiring engineering validation in the actual airframe.
